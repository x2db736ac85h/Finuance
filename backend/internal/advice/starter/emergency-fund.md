An emergency fund is money kept aside for the unexpected: a broken boiler, a car repair, a gap between jobs. Its job is to keep a surprise from turning into debt.

**How much.** A common starting point is one month of essential spending (rent, food, transport, bills), growing towards three to six months. Look at what you actually spend: the Overview shows your monthly spending, and the categories you could not cut in a hard month are your essentials.

**Where.** Somewhere separate from your everyday account, easy to reach within a day or two, and not invested in anything that can fall in value just when you need it.

**How to get there.**

- Start small. A first target of one week of spending is still a cushion.
- Pay yourself first: move a fixed amount right after payday instead of saving what is left at the end of the month.
- Put windfalls (a bonus, a tax refund, a sold item) towards it.
- Set a savings goal with a deadline so you can see whether you are on pace.

**When to use it.** For things that are necessary, unexpected and urgent. A holiday or a sale is not an emergency; a goal of its own suits those better. After using the fund, rebuild it before saving for anything else.
