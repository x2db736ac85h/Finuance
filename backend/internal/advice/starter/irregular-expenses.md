title: Planning for expenses that don't come every month
tags: budgeting, planning, yearly
---
Car insurance, a yearly subscription, school costs, birthdays and holidays: they are predictable, just not monthly. They wreck a budget when they arrive in the same month.

**List them.** Go through the last twelve months of transactions and note every payment that happened once or a few times a year. Yearly recurring payments are a good start; then look for seasonal spending such as gifts in December or travel in summer.

**Spread them out.** Add up the yearly total and divide by twelve. Setting that amount aside every month (in a separate account or as a savings goal) turns a lumpy year into a flat one. When the bill comes, the money is already there.

**Give them a budget.** A category such as "Gifts" or "Car" with a monthly budget shows the true monthly cost, even in months without a payment.

**Use groups for one-off projects.** A renovation or a trip can be a transaction group: the group window shows what it cost in total and per day, which helps when planning the next one.

**Leave a margin.** Prices rise and some years have surprises. Rounding the monthly amount up a little is cheaper than a short-notice loan.
