title: "Paying down debt: highest rate first, or smallest balance first"
tags: debt, loans, credit cards
---
With more than one debt, pay the minimum on all of them, then put every extra amount on one debt at a time. There are two common ways to choose which.

**Highest interest rate first** (often called the avalanche). You pay the least interest overall, because the most expensive debt shrinks first. It suits you if you can stay motivated without quick wins.

**Smallest balance first** (often called the snowball). The first debt disappears quickly, and the payment you no longer make rolls onto the next one. You may pay a bit more interest, but many people keep going because they see progress.

Either beats spreading the extra thinly across all debts.

**Before you start.**

- List every debt with its balance, interest rate and minimum payment.
- Keep a small emergency fund, so a surprise does not go on a credit card again.
- Check whether a debt has early-repayment fees, and whether a lower-rate consolidation is honestly cheaper once the fees are counted.

**Tracking it.** A savings goal can track an amount you want to put towards a debt by a date; spending less on one or two categories frees the money for it.

If repayments already take more than you can cover, talk to the lender early or to a free debt advice service in your country.
