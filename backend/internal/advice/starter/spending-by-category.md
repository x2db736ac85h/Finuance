title: Finding out where your money goes

Before changing anything, look at what happened. A few months of transactions sorted into categories show more than any plan.

**Get the categories right first.** The bank's categories are a start, but they are often too broad or wrong for shops that sell many things. Rules recategorise a payee everywhere, now and for future imports, so fixing a frequent payee once is worth it. Transfers between your own accounts are not spending, so they are left out of the charts.

**Look at three numbers per category.**

- The monthly average over several months, not just the last one.
- The trend: is it creeping up?
- The few largest transactions: one big purchase can hide a modest habit, or the other way round.

**Look at the small, frequent ones.** Coffee, snacks, taxis and delivery fees are small enough to ignore one by one; the payees list and the daily spending chart show how often they happen.

**Decide what to change.** Pick one or two categories, not ten. A monthly budget a little below the current average, or a goal to spend a percentage less, is easier to keep than a big cut.

**Check again** after a month. Progress shows in the averages, not in one good week.
