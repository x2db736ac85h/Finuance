title: "A simple split for your income: needs, wants, savings"

If budgeting category by category feels like too much, start with three buckets for your take-home pay:

- **Needs**: what you must pay whatever happens: housing, utilities, groceries, transport to work, minimum debt payments, insurance.
- **Wants**: everything you choose: eating out, hobbies, subscriptions, travel, most shopping.
- **Savings and extra debt payments**: the emergency fund, goals, paying more than the minimum on a loan.

A popular rule of thumb is roughly half for needs, a third for wants and a fifth for savings. Treat the numbers as a starting point, not a test: where rent is high, needs take more, and that is normal. What matters is that the savings bucket is never empty by default.

**Using your own numbers.** Spending by category tells you where you are today. Add up the categories that are needs, then the wants, and compare them with your income for the same months. The first comparison is often surprising; it is information, not a verdict.

**Turning it into budgets.** Give the biggest want categories a monthly budget a little below what you spend now. The budget card on the Overview shows when a category is ahead of pace for the month, early enough to change course.

**Reviewing it.** Look again after a few months, or when your income or rent changes.
